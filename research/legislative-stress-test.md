## Transfer contract
Goal: detect computable defects in proposed or amended rules before public decisions, without equating a proof with a legal interpretation. Existing Warrant separates authority, evidence and assurance. The new synthetic benefit model is the cheapest discriminating test; not a fiscal forecast. Domain: integer monthly earnings 0–2000; v1 from 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31; v2 from 2027-01-01 through 2027-12-31. Exact dollars. Prediction: one adjacent decrease in v1, none in v2; alternatives: additional decreases or a remaining cliff. Separate oracle counts individually retained dollars, rather than reusing the clamp expression. Stop after exhaustive replay and normal release gates. No novelty claim.

## Reusable mechanisms / implementation queue
1. Temporal rule ledger: stable provision ID, jurisdiction, official source URI, content digest, enactment date, effective interval, repeal/amendment lineage, retrieval timestamp. Resolve as-of date; missing and competing rules fail closed. Use separate knowledge-time vs valid-time for retrospective corrections. Proposed, not live.
2. Dependency lint: resolve citations against the applicable version; distinguish intended historical references, unresolved references, ambiguity and actual defects. Recheck reverse dependency closure on amendment; keep an explicit allowlist with evidence and expiry. Proposed.
3. Typed shared rule kernel: money units, caps, thresholds, eligibility, rounding, exceptions, precedence and aggregation. Model unknown inputs explicitly; never treat unknown law as zero. First fixture implements two dated benefit rules only.
4. Translation verification: freeze sources and specification, compare against official worked examples and independent implementations, use preregistered hidden holdouts; reconcile mismatches against applicable source, not majority vote. Reviewer must not author the translation. No independent legal review here.
5. Policy invariants: net-resource monotonicity, consistent treatment of equivalent cases, nonnegative quantities where required, boundary behavior and budget constraints. These are hypotheses, not automatic legal requirements. A witness can reflect intended policy, model error or unintended consequences; preserve classification and dispute.
6. Search at structural boundaries first (threshold +/- one unit, date transitions, rounding, rule interactions), then bounded exhaustive tests or proof; no-counterexample is not an unrestricted theorem. Fixture scans every adjacent integer pair.
7. Amendment regression: compare before/after witnesses, intended changes vs unexpected changes; freeze hashes, test suites, source map and model coverage. CI must pass before deployment. Future legal reviews and Lean proofs have separate statuses.
8. Warrant evidence record: provision/source IDs and hashes, rule version, inputs, computed outputs, property, witness, checker revision and scope; legal authority and interpretation separately assessed. Never grant governmental authority through a check.

## First fixture
v1 grant = 100 if income <= 1000, else 0. v2 grant = clamp(1100-income, 0, 100). Net resources = earnings + grant. v1 has a unique adjacent decrease 1000 -> 1001: 1100 -> 1001 (-99). v2 has none on the stated integer domain (flat over the phaseout). This removes the modeled cliff, not all economic disincentives; it also expands eligibility and potentially raises expenditure. No population or fiscal data is available. No claim of optimal policy.

Replay: node scripts/test_legislative_stress.mjs; static gate: python3 scripts/test_standalone.py .; standard browser CI. Source module commonwealth-legislative-stress.mjs. Public example is local-only and fictional. At initial release, independent human review and Lean certification had not been performed. Future promotion requires authentic jurisdictional sources, interpretation review, a pinned specification, externally checked examples and approval.

## Formalization extension (2026-11-24)

The formal model now generalizes unit-slope phaseouts to arbitrary natural-number thresholds, amounts and income; unique-cliff and monotonicity theorems have no 2,000-dollar limit. The executable site remains intentionally bounded. The formal calendar models 2026 and 2027 only; the bridge tests every year/month/day tuple in 2025–2028 × 0–13 × 0–32, with invalid tuples rejected. String syntax, leap years outside the supported period and browser behavior are not universally formalized. No dependency graph is executed by this demo, so no amendment-closure claim is made. Kernel and cross-language replay are release gates. The two preceding runs failed because diagnostics were hidden and then because the version dispatch had not been reduced before a split; the statement was unchanged, and the corrected run passed.
